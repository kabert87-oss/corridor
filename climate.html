Issue 031 · 21 September 2026 · Continental

Africa sells its carbon at $3 a tonne. The same tonne is worth up to sixty times more once it leaves the continent.

Africa owns the forest, yet it is others who set the price of its carbon. The continent holds some of the largest carbon sinks on earth, and a fast-growing market pays it to keep them standing, but that market sets the price elsewhere. The African Development Bank's own former president has said foreign buyers sometimes pay as little as three dollars a tonne for African carbon, while a comparable permit in Europe can cost around two hundred. He called it a carbon grab. This issue asks who prices Africa's natural capital, and where the value goes once the credit is sold.
