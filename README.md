# The Corridor Africa

**A weekly publication of record on African tourism and the world that shapes it.**

Published every Monday from Nairobi. Read by tourism ministries, development finance institutions, hotel investors and the analysts who advise them.

Live at [thecorridorafrica.com](https://thecorridorafrica.com)

## What this is

The Corridor Africa is an analytical publication covering the intersection of African tourism and global political economy. Each issue takes a single question, grounds it in primary sources, and reads it through a consistent analytical frame. The work is aimed at a policy and investment audience: the World Bank, the African Development Bank, UN Tourism, and the institutions whose decisions shape how value moves through African economies.

The recurring question across the series is one of economic sovereignty. Who owns the assets, who captures the value they generate, and who sets the terms on which they are sold. That question has been traced through hotels, airlines, booking platforms, coastlines, visa regimes, domestic tourism markets, and carbon.

Six analytical departments, each with its own framework:

- **Policy & Governance** — Sovereign Tourism Architecture
- **Climate & Environment** — Climate Re-routing
- **Diplomacy & Trade** — Sovereign Signalling

## Regional desks

East, Southern, West, North and Central Africa, alongside continental issues that cut across all five.

## Analytical contributions

The publication has introduced several named concepts to describe mechanisms it documents, each situated in the existing scholarly literature rather than claimed as new theory:

- **Convertibility bias** — the tendency of tourism measurement systems to record hard-currency activity and overlook the local-currency market.
- **Enforced disintermediation** — the contractual mechanism by which online travel platforms make themselves permanent intermediaries, so an operator can never convert a platform-acquired guest into a direct one.
- **The ownership-control gap** — the separation, by long contract, of the party that owns and finances a tourism asset from the party that controls it and captures its recurring value.

## Editorial standards

Every issue is built to a consistent bar. Claims rest on primary sources, government data, institutional filings and peer-reviewed work rather than secondary reporting. Each argument is situated in its existing literature. Every issue includes a section that states the honest limit of its own argument. Figures are attributed and dated.
